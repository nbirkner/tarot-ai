# Reading

## Scope

Persuade on the landing. Operate on the draw: question, context, deck, spread, photo, chart, loading, reveal, result, errors, and the cap. History and about use the same world.

## Audience and job

A visitor decides to draw, then finishes a reading. Proof is the deck itself: geometric faces, then their own generated art and text.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A pocket oracle printed like a modern grimoire, ember field and black ink, refusing the cream-and-serif tarot template and the parent site's quiet editorial system.
OWN-WORLD: Drenched #FF5A36, ink #140C0A, paper #FFF6F0. Host Grotesk uppercase display with italic stress. Azeret Mono for indexes and labels. Sigil wordmark. Thin rules. Deck rows, spread tiles, and cards with a hard offset shadow and a slight turn.
STORY: The visitor reads the poster, starts a reading, sets an intention, draws, turns the cards, and gets a thought from the spread. A limit stops them in the same voice.
FIRST VIEWPORT: Full ember field. Masthead is sigil plus tarotai, a center line, and Oracle, History, About. The headline "Your future called. It has notes." sits large and left, italic on the second sentence, with the tagline and a cream-on-ink Begin Your Reading control. A fan of three geometric cards and the sample thought share that first view on a wide screen and follow on a phone.
FORM: Brief-pinned Neo-Grimoire (v0 Direction C). No concept roll. The approved mock is the critique reference. Code-led, because the mock is an exploration screen and the product is the whole multi-step app.
FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance.

## Memorable moment

The deal and the flip: cards land slightly crooked with a hard shadow, then spring over to the generated art.

## Constraints

Do not change limiter logic, API limit checks, model calls, or env handling. Title Case on labels and controls. No emoji, stock photos, or em dashes in chrome. Mobile first. Focus and reduced motion.
